- Cut first-install wall time ~3x (freeze ~20x) by dropping the 37,999 per-entry `fsync`s from `freeze()`: freeze now chmods entries read-only in bounded batches and issues one filesystem-wide flush (Linux: `sync -f`, ~2 s measured vs ~110 s of per-file fsync transactions; missing binary or other platforms degrade to a slower copy, never an error), and reads the built tree once for its manifest. Crash safety is unchanged and restated in `docs/runtime-kits.md`: the manifest is a claim that pre-publication `verifyEngineSet` proves against every byte on disk, manifest writes stay durable (`atomic()` fsyncs `.byokit-tree`/`.byokit-patches`), the publication rename keeps its `syncDir(sets)` fsync, and after a power loss a torn published set mismatches its manifest and is rebuilt as drift — never launched. On every non-Linux platform (macOS, Windows and BSD included) there is no bulk flush, so durability there rests on that same next-launch whole-tree verification; the worst case is a full reinstall, never a launched torn set.
